The in-domain metric saturates where transfer begins

Split each non-thinking run in half. Over the first 5,120 rollouts MATH-500 gains +2.2 points and AIME gains nothing (−0.7, not significant). Over the second 5,120 the pattern inverts: MATH-500 adds +0.1 and AIME adds +5.0. The same reversal appears in all three non-thinking bands, at p = 0.008 / 0.002 / 0.009 — three independent replications of the same effect.

Stopping at 5,120 rollouts because MATH-500 had flattened would have produced the conclusion that nothing transferred.

For the student, only the hard band transfers

On MATH-500 Llama's three bands are indistinguishable — +5.0, +5.3, +6.0, with intervals that overlap almost entirely; if anything the easy band is best. On AIME they separate. The hard band rises at every checkpoint (12.0 → 12.8 → 14.5 → 16.0 → 17.0, +4.9, p = 0.018) and is still rising when the budget runs out. The medium and easy bands give +2.2 and +1.8, neither distinguishable from zero.

Difficulty does not change how much a model gains on the distribution it trained on. It changes whether the gain leaves that distribution.

HMMT cannot resolve anything for a 3B model

Llama's HMMT baseline is 1.8% pass@4 — 10 correct samples out of 1,488, spread over 4 of 93 problems. Solving two more problems doubles the metric, and all three bands wander between 1.5% and 4.1% with no trend. It is reported for completeness, not as evidence. HMMT separates the two Qwen configurations cleanly (20.5% against 57.0%), which is what it was added for.
