A pilot, not a converged comparison. 20 optimisation steps is where standard GRPO recipes use hundreds. These curves answer whether movement happens and whether it differs by band. Several are still rising at the last point.

  • The two matched controls disagree about the confound's sign. Group size ties to the band, so at a fixed 512 rollouts per step the middle bands also covered four times as many distinct problems. Re-running them at matched group size — the dashed lines — hurt Llama on AIME (+2.2 to +0.1) and helped non-thinking on all three (AIME +4.0 to +5.5), and left thinking where it was (AIME +2.2 to −0.2, still nothing). With no consistent direction, those differences are inside what a five-point curve resolves — and no control overturns anything.
  • The thinking rows carry a confound that cannot be removed. Only 851 problems in the whole 12k pool have 0 < pass@1 < 1 for this configuration, so its hard band is 100 problems seen 3.2 times while its easy band is 400 seen 0.8 times. Equalising them would mean throwing away three quarters of the larger bands.
  • One cell is incomplete. The thinking hard band ran out of GPU memory at step 19 of 20 and has no 10,240-rollout endpoint; its last point is ringed and is not comparable with the other endpoints.
  • Evaluation has real run-to-run variance. Two runs of the identical AIME evaluation over the identical 150 problems returned pass@4 15.3% and 12.0% — vLLM's batching makes even a fixed seed non-reproducible across shard layouts. Every interval quoted here bootstraps over generations as well as problems for that reason; resampling problems alone understates them by about a third.
  • Difficulty is measured at 8 or 32 samples, and training draws 32 fresh ones every step. A band label is therefore a noisy estimate of the model's rate on those problems, not a property of them. This bites hardest at the edges: "never solved in 8 samples" misses a true 4.7% rate two thirds of the time, which is why the p = 0 bands train at all.
  • Three problems in the 11,996-pool have an empty answer — the upstream extraction failed on their multiple-choice form. They can never be scored correct, so they sort into p = 0 by construction rather than by being hard. They are dropped at conversion.
  • Llama's training pool excludes contaminated problems. It scores 61.0% on problems from the original MATH train split against 40.3% on test-derived ones, so its bands draw only from the 4,498 clean problems. The Qwen bands use the full pool, where no such asymmetry is detectable.
