The in-domain metric saturates where transfer begins

Split each non-thinking run in half. Over the first 5,120 rollouts MATH-500 gains +2.2 points and AIME gains nothing (−0.7, not significant). Over the second 5,120 the pattern inverts: MATH-500 adds +0.1 and AIME adds +5.0. The same reversal appears in all three non-thinking bands, at p = 0.008 / 0.002 / 0.009 — three independent replications of one effect.

An early-stop rule watching MATH-500 flatten would have stopped at the halfway point and concluded that nothing transferred.

The two models disagree about whether difficulty matters

For Llama, MATH-500 cannot tell the bands apart — +5.0, +6.3, +6.0, intervals almost fully overlapping, the easy band nominally highest. AIME can. The hard band rises at every checkpoint (12.0 → 12.8 → 14.5 → 16.0 → 17.0, +4.9, p = 0.018) and is still rising when the budget ends; a specific monotone ordering has probability 1/24 under no trend. The medium band, re-run with its group size matched to the extremes so only difficulty differs, goes to +0.1 — up to 16.0 and back down to 12.1, ending where it started.

For Qwen3-4B non-thinking, matched the same way, every band transfers at similar magnitude: AIME +4.3 / +5.5 / +4.8 across hard, medium and easy, with the medium band the strongest cell overall. Under pass@1 — the stricter reading, in the table's last column — all four non-thinking cells clear zero on both transfer benchmarks, which pass@4 does not show: pass@4 rewards coverage and is the noisier statistic, so its intervals are wider.

The two configurations differ in nearly everything that could explain this — 39.1% against 83.4% on MATH-500, an AIME baseline of 12.0% near the floor against 36.0% that is not, and a band called “hard” meaning 5–15% pass@1 for one and 12–25% for the other. This data supports no difficulty-band prescription that carries across models. What it does show is that the question is invisible in-domain: on MATH-500 the bands are indistinguishable for both configurations.

The strongest configuration cannot be taught this way at all

Qwen3-4B in thinking mode moves on nothing. Across three bands and three benchmarks, every interval covers zero: MATH-500 −0.1 to +0.1, AIME −0.1 to +2.2, HMMT +0.4 to +1.9. Under pass@1 its three AIME deltas are −0.5, −1.7 and −3.0 — still inside the noise, but pointing down rather than up. The training itself ran normally. There is simply nothing for plain GRPO to hold on to — the model already scores 95.9% on MATH-500, and 92.9% of the 12k training pool produces no gradient at all, because every rollout in a group is correct or every one is wrong. Its three bands are cut from the 851 problems that remain.

This is the gap the project exists to probe. On-policy sampling can only teach what the model already sometimes gets right; where it does not, the signal is not weak but absent, and a teacher's trajectory is the only thing that could supply one.

HMMT cannot resolve anything for a 3B model

Llama's HMMT baseline is 1.8% pass@4 — 10 correct samples out of 1,488, spread over 4 of 93 problems. Solving two more problems doubles the metric, and all three bands wander between 1.5% and 4.1% with no trend. It is reported for completeness, not as evidence. HMMT separates the two Qwen configurations cleanly (20.5% against 57.0%), which is what it was added for.
